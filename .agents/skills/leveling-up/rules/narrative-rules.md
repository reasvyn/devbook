# Leveling Up — Narrative Rules

This file contains rules specific to the `level-up/` subject. General content rules apply from [CONTENT-RULES.md](../../../../CONTENT-RULES.md), with the style exceptions described below.

## Writing Style: Literary Nonfiction

`level-up/` uses a **literary nonfiction** style — prose that applies the techniques of fiction (vivid scenes, sensory detail, emotional depth, pacing) to real experience and real knowledge. The result reads like a novel but teaches like a textbook.

This style sits in a specific middle ground:

| Too little (avoid) | The target zone | Too much (avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Academic treatise | Literary nonfiction | Pure fiction |
| Clinical detachment | Immersive prose | Invented plot |
| List of instructions | Scene-driven narrative | Fixed fictional cast |
| Abstract theory | Lived experience | Escapist fantasy |

### What this means in practice

**The prose is literary.** Write with the same attention to rhythm, imagery, and emotional resonance that a novelist brings to fiction. Use scenes, not summaries. Show the moment of despair — the keyboard glows at 3 AM, the cursor blinks on an empty terminal, the coffee is cold. Then show the moment of breakthrough. The writing should move the reader, not just inform them.

**The perspective is flexible.** There are no restrictions on pronouns or point of view. Use first person when the moment demands intimacy. Use second person when the reader must be placed inside the experience. Use third person when distance or observation serves the point. Shift freely between perspectives within a single document if the writing benefits from it. The only constraint is clarity — the reader must never be confused about who is speaking or to whom.

**There are no fixed characters.** The reader is the protagonist of their own journey, but the documents may include any number of voices: composite characters drawn from real patterns, hypothetical individuals who illustrate a point, historical figures whose experience illuminates the present, or unnamed "you" and "I" that carry the reader through a moment. Characters serve the knowledge. They appear when the point needs a face and disappear when the point is made.

**The content is real.** Despite the literary techniques, nothing in `level-up/` is invented for dramatic effect. The scenarios are drawn from real developer experience. The psychological patterns are grounded in research. The advice is practical and applicable. The writing may use composite characters or hypothetical examples, but the underlying truth must be verifiable in the real world.

**The tone is direct and honest.** No padding, no fluff, no false reassurance. The writing acknowledges difficulty without dramatizing it. It offers hope without promising easy resolution. It respects the reader's intelligence and experience.

## Core Principles

- **Literary, not academic.** `level-up/` uses the prose techniques of fiction to explore real experience. It does NOT teach academic knowledge through academic prose. The theory lives in other DevBook subjects. Here, we walk the path.
- **Reference, don't duplicate.** Every file links to other DevBook subjects for the actual knowledge and theoretical grounding. Use Prerequisites and Next Steps to cross-reference broadly — philosophy, psychology, biology, neuroscience, sociology, or any subject that provides depth.
- **Open-ended journey.** New modules, themes, and stages may emerge as the journey deepens. Do not assume the current structure is final. The journey is the destination.

## Code Examples

Code examples are **optional**. The prose is primary. Use code only when it serves as a metaphor or illustration that prose cannot achieve as effectively. Never force code into a section where it feels artificial.
